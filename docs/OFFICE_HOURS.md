# Office Hours diagnostic

**Mode:** startup, pre-product. This is a hypothesis document; no customer demand is claimed.

## Specific customer

The initial buyer is a data-program lead at a warehouse or industrial robotics company who owns a deployed manipulation model and has a specific task with poor success rates. The initial supplier is a licensed industrial electrician already doing repeatable shop-floor work at an employer willing to authorize capture.

## Painful status quo

The buyer currently commissions a general data vendor, stages an artificial lab, or sends internal staff to collect footage. The result is expensive coordination and lots of visually plausible footage that lacks task steps, outcomes, recovery behavior, or clean rights. The worker receives no upside and bears privacy and safety risk.

## Desperate use case

A buyer needs 100+ clean demonstrations of a narrow task—control-panel terminal wiring—with tool/object labels, failures, and recovery sequences before a model-training milestone. A delay blocks the next evaluation cycle.

## Narrowest paid wedge

Sell one fixed-scope, buyer-funded capture pilot: one site, one skilled worker, one task ontology, 60–120 minutes of raw recording, segmented clips, consent proof, privacy review, and an acceptance report. Do not launch a broad marketplace first.

## Observation and validation

1. Interview five robotics data leads using a concrete control-panel capture brief; ask for their current spec and rejection criteria.
2. Observe two authorized workers performing the task without coaching; map unplanned setup, exceptions, and safety boundaries.
3. Pre-sell one $2,500–$7,500 pilot before building ingestion infrastructure.
4. Success gate: a buyer supplies a task spec, agrees to acceptance criteria, and pays a deposit; a site and worker consent to capture.

## Future-fit

As robot models become more capable, generic footage becomes less valuable and coverage of rare, high-consequence edge cases becomes more valuable. WorkTrace survives if it owns trusted task ontologies, contributor operations, rights lineage, and buyer-specific acceptance history. It fails if synthetic data eliminates demand for grounded edge cases or hardware vendors internalize capture.

## Approaches considered

- **A — open footage directory (minimal build):** fastest software, but produces supply before demand and weak quality control.
- **B — managed task pilot (recommended):** manually recruit one worker/site against a buyer brief; lowest marketplace risk and strongest learning.
- **C — full capture network (ideal architecture):** contributor app, devices, automated labeling, QA, licensing, and marketplace; premature before repeat demand.
