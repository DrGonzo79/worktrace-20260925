# CEO review

## Verdict

The raw “directory of trade videos” premise is weak: it attracts speculative supply, races toward commodity pricing, and ignores employer/site rights. The investable wedge is a managed, buyer-funded task capture with explicit acceptance criteria.

## Challenges and decisions

- **Premise:** More video is not the goal; accepted edge-case demonstrations are. Changed the product from directory browsing to capture planning.
- **ICP:** “Robotics companies” was too broad. Narrowed to the data-program lead accountable for one industrial manipulation task.
- **GTM:** Two-sided marketplace launch creates a cold start. Demand is recruited first; supply follows a funded brief.
- **Economics:** Per-hour source pricing hides rejected footage and operations. The plan tracks accepted-minute margin and sets a kill threshold.
- **Moat:** Smart glasses and generic labeling are purchasable. Accepted task ontologies, contributor/site trust and rights lineage are the defensible compounding assets.
- **Architecture:** Video scale does not justify early platform complexity. Begin with signed uploads and human QA; add automated proposals only after rejection data exists.
- **Observability:** Averages conceal bad collections. Instrument rejection reason by task, worker, site, device and model version; alert on consent gaps and acceptance regression.
- **Failure modes:** Privacy, safety, ownership and schema mismatch can kill the company before model quality matters. Delivery must fail closed without complete consent and review.

## What would change the decision

If buyers repeatedly purchase generic pre-existing footage without custom specs, test a catalog. If two pilots require entirely incompatible schemas and no repeatable ontology emerges, stop building a marketplace and remain a services firm—or stop entirely.
