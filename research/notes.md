## Useful source claims, summarized

The source argues that robotics investment is accelerating while real-world task performance remains poor because training data is thin. Its proposed supply model pays skilled workers to record first-person work, then cleans, segments, and labels footage for robotics buyers. It emphasizes underserved tasks such as trailer loading, returns, pallet teardown, electrical wiring, inspections, plumbing, painting, drywall, and mechanic work rather than household chores.

The source says raw generic footage is becoming commodity-priced while targeted teleoperation or task-specific data can command roughly $50–$200 per hour. Those numbers are directional source claims, not validated unit economics. WorkTrace uses fictional rates inside that band strictly to demonstrate the workflow.

## Product interpretation

The valuable wedge is not an open directory of random footage. It is a buyer-funded capture brief for one task ontology, with consent, safety, privacy, coverage, and acceptance criteria defined before anyone records. The prototype therefore begins with task qualification and a paid-pilot plan.
